# Economics Extrapolations — Relaxation Formula

## What These Are

The economics extrapolations apply the relaxation rate law to economic and financial systems. They are **speculative** — not tests of the formula, but explorations of what the formula predicts when the "niche" is a market, the "organism" is a firm or career, and "capacity" is skill diversity or portfolio breadth.

The formula: **dρ/dt = −k₁(ρ − ρ₁) − k₂(ρ − ρ₂)**

where ρ is retained capability, the attractor ρ_eq is the equilibrium capability set for the committed niche, and k is the rate of capability shedding (inversely proportional to integration depth).

---

## Extrapolation 1 — Firm Specialization (Career Narrowing)

**What it explores:** When a firm commits to a market niche, it sheds generalized capabilities. The rate of capability loss is proportional to the mismatch between carried capabilities and market demands.

**What to look for:** Firm capability breadth follows bi-exponential decay after niche commitment. Fast phase: shedding capabilities irrelevant to the niche. Slow phase: erosion of core capabilities as the niche saturates.

**Prediction:** Track firm capability diversity (number of distinct business lines, skill diversity of workforce) over time since founding. Bi-exponential fit should win over linear or logistic.

---

## Extrapolation 2 — Credit and Banking Upcycles

**What it explores:** Credit volume expands during the upcycle as P_K (capital goods price) exceeds P_c (consumer goods price). The mismatch between credit volume and real-economy demand generates a relaxation dynamic.

**What to look for:** Credit volume approaches its equilibrium at a rate proportional to the mismatch. The upcycle is the fast phase (k₁); the mature cycle is the slow phase (k₂). The collapse (Ponzi → Speculative → Hedge) is the re-coupling of credit firms to real-economy firms when the mismatch reverses.

**Prediction:** Fit bi-exponential to credit volume time series during expansion phases. The rate constant k₁ should correlate with the magnitude of P_K − P_c.

---

## Extrapolation 3 — Technology Adoption and Skill Obsolescence

**What it explores:** When a new technology creates a niche (e.g., software engineering), early entrants carry broad skill sets. As the niche matures, the skill set narrows: specialized roles emerge, and generalist skills are shed.

**What to look for:** Skill diversity within a profession follows bi-exponential decay from founding. The fast phase is the first 5-10 years (rapid specialization); the slow phase is decades (entrenchment).

**Prediction:** Track skill diversity in professional cohorts (e.g., software engineers, 1980-2025) using job posting data.

---

## Extrapolation 4 — Option Value Collapse

**What it explores:** Financial options have time value that decays as expiration approaches. The relaxation formula predicts: if the "niche" is the commitment to holding the position, the rate of capacity (optionality) loss is proportional to the mismatch between carried risk and the niche's risk structure.

**What to look for:** Option time value collapse is bi-exponential, not mono-exponential, when the position is deep in the money (high mismatch → fast decay) vs at the money (low mismatch → slow decay).

**Prediction:** Compare bi-exponential vs mono-exponential theta models on options data near and far from the money.
