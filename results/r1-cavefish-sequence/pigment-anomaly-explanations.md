# Pigment Anomaly — Explanations (Jan directive, 2026-09-05)

**The anomaly, precisely:** in the R1 rebuild (T21), pigment regresses early and
nearly completely (0.95 by 20 kya) despite the *highest* documented mean STRING
degree of any trait (4.4) vs eye 1.44, sleep 0.0. Naive VI ("deeper integration →
lost later") predicts pigment LAST; it went early.

**Anchoring facts (established, sourced):**
- Melanophore LINEAGE is retained — melanoblasts migrate normally, are
  tyrosinase-positive, cannot synthesize melanin; exogenous L-DOPA rescues it
  (Jeffery 2009; PMC3594791).
- oca2 loss blocks L-tyrosine uptake into melanosomes → elevates L-tyrosine,
  dopamine, norepinephrine in larvae and adult brains; oca2 suppression in
  surface embryos reproduces the elevation (Bilandžija et al. 2013, PLoS ONE
  e0080823). Elevated catecholamines drive adaptive cave behaviors (reduced
  sleep, altered feeding, light-evoked photokinesis).
- OCA2 loss-of-function recurs independently across cave populations; different
  mutations (Pachón vs Molino complementation) (Jeffery 2009).
- Eye regression is driven by antagonistic pleiotropy with constructive traits
  (VAB/neuromast QTL clusters; Yoshizawa 2012).

## SATISFIES VI (reallocation readings)

### 1. Keystone-node reallocation at the tyrosine fork — SATISFIES
The melanin/catecholamine fork is literally zero-sum: tyrosine → melanin XOR
dopamine/norepinephrine. Pigment loss isn't shedding; it *funds* the new niche's
neurochemistry.
**Established pattern:** metabolic channeling at precursor forks (e.g., tryptophan
→ serotonin vs kynurenine; phenylalanine shunting in melanogenic tissues).
**Read:** this IS VI's core mechanism — capacity reallocation through a
developmental trade-off. High degree is exactly why the node is a good lever.

### 2. Antagonistic pleiotropy with constructive traits — SATISFIES
Regression as the flip side of gain: documented for eyes (VAB/neuromasts,
Yoshizawa 2012); same logic for pigment via catecholamine-linked behaviors.
**Established pattern:** antagonistic pleiotropy is ubiquitous (SHH expansion →
jaw/taste gain + eye loss; color-signal vs immunity tradeoffs).
**Read:** pigment loss is a byproduct of constructive selection — niche
absorption, fully VI.

### 3. Effective (in-niche) integration collapses — SATISFIES
Camouflage, UV protection, color signaling are all dead in aphotic caves.
Pigment's *effective* demand ≈ 0 even with high ancestral PPI degree.
**Established pattern:** relaxed-selection environments nullify specific functions
(island birds lose flight, cave fauna lose pigment) — the whole troglobite
literature.
**Read:** VI is about capacity relative to *niche demand*; zero demand → gone
early regardless of wiring. The metric (surface-context STRING) is the wrong
quantity, not the theory.

### 4. Module-retention reframe — SATISFIES (dissolves the anomaly)
What is shed is the shallow *terminal module* (melanin synthesis); what is
retained is the deeply integrated *lineage* (neural-crest melanoblasts).
**Established pattern:** evolution preferentially deletes terminal steps of
biosynthetic pathways while conserving upstream developmental programs (e.g.,
vitamin C synthesis loss in primates — terminal enzyme gone, pathway upstream
intact; adult lactase variation).
**Read:** measurement unit is wrong, not prediction. "Trait" ≠ "module." The
anomaly was generated by scoring whole traits.

### 5. Direct energetics in resource-poor caves — SATISFIES
Melanin synthesis costs; food is scarce; dropping synthesis saves energy AND
donates precursor to the high-yield catecholamine pathway.
**Established pattern:** cost-of-trait arguments (energetic savings of reduced
flight apparatus, costly-tissue hypothesis for gut/brain).
**Read:** directional cost-shedding, literal. VI names this mechanism.

### 6. Correlated-regression syndrome (shared developmental axis) — SATISFIES
Eyes and pigment regress together across cave taxa — the classic "eyeless,
depigmented troglobite" syndrome.
**Established pattern:** widespread — cave shrimp, crayfish, salamanders, fish
all lose both.
**Read:** VI-compatible if the shared axis (neural crest / early development)
is the reallocated capacity; pigment's early timing rides the same developmental
channel as eye. (Metric caveat: scoring traits as independent units breaks — but
the theory handles it.)

## CHALLENGES VI

### 7. Control-point leverage inversion — CHALLENGES
High degree = best *lever*, not anchor: one gene, maximal channeling. Evolution
pulls hubs for coordinated multi-trait reconfiguration at minimal genetic cost.
**Established pattern:** hub genes as evolvability levers (toolkit genes: pax6,
shh, hox); mutations in hubs driving coordinated change.
**Read:** fatal to the one-axis metric — "integration depth" is two-sided
(retention vs leverage); the naive predictor has the wrong sign for hubs.

### 8. Mutation-supply / target-size ordering — CHALLENGES (strongest threat)
OCA2 is a large gene (exon deletions) — a big mutational target. In small
founder populations, loss-of-function fixes early as near-neutral; the
catecholamine benefit co-opts/retains it later. Same few genes hit across
independent lineages (complementation) = hot-spot signature, not integration.
**Established pattern:** convergent gene-level loss in cave taxa (oca2 in
Astyanax; same loci across cave lineages in isopods, beetles); mutation-target
size effects on fixation rates.
**Read:** loss order may track mutation supply + drift, not integration at all.
Directly threatens the T17-style ordering claim. Testable.

### 9. Heterogeneity of regressive mechanisms — CHALLENGES (identifiability)
Different traits are lost by different mechanisms (relaxation vs direct
selection vs indirect/pleiotropic). If so, a single global ordering claim (one
metric, one axis) is not identifiable from loss-order data.
**Established pattern:** the regressive-evolution literature explicitly
distinguishes these mechanisms; exceptions abound (Molino anomaly in our own
E7 data).
**Read:** "loss order" may not be one well-defined dependent variable. The
anomaly is the diagnostic that exposes it.

### 10. Pre-existing relaxed state / standing variation — CHALLENGES (adjudication)
Surface Astyanax vary in pigmentation; a partially relaxed pigment state may
predate colonization — pigment becomes a "head-start" trait whose order was set
before the niche shift.
**Established pattern:** standing variation fueling rapid parallel adaptation
(and relaxation) generally.
**Read:** the cave data cannot adjudicate VI's ordering claim *for pigment* —
its order is confounded by pre-adaptation state.

## Synthesis: two-axis metric (the diagnostic takeaway)

The anomaly says VI needs two orthogonal axes:
- **A. Retention depth** (what survives): GRN position, lineage necessity,
  developmental-role count.
- **B. Lever value** (what goes first): reallocation yield in the new niche —
  substrate convertibility, antagonistic connections, terminal-module
  dispensability.

Pigment: deep on A (lineage retained in every population), high on B (synthesis
terminal shed first, funding catecholamine behavior). Eye: deep on A (but
completely lost — Dollo), high on B. Sleep: shallow on A (hcrt), high on B.
A one-axis operationalization (STRING ≈ A) fails exactly where B drives order —
the pigment cell. Falsifiable refinement, not a hole.

## Discriminating tests (priority order)

1. **Catecholamine timing across populations:** does dopamine/norepinephrine
   elevation precede pigment loss (channeling: satisfies 1) or follow it
   (co-optation: challenges 8)?
2. **Recurrence + target size:** same high-target genes across all independent
   lineages? Does loss timing correlate with founder size (→8) or not (→1)?
3. **Melanoblast retention across all populations:** if yes universally, A is
   deep → 4 confirmed, anomaly dissolves into measurement error.
4. **Partial-light caves:** residual light → less pigment loss predicts 3;
   no change predicts 1/5 (channeling is light-independent).
5. **Surface standing variation:** pre-existing relaxation predicts 10.
6. **GRN dependency depth (not PPI degree):** if oca2 is functionally shallow
   in the shed module, one axis survives → 4.
